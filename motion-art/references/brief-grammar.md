# Brief grammar

A brief is 70–120 words of plain, concrete prose, 5–7 sentences. It is written for a painter,
and every sentence should be something the code can do.

## Shape

1. **Subject and framing.** The object, its state and setting, and the viewpoint or crop.
   *"A folding aluminium lawn chair … seen at noon from a low three-quarter angle."*
2. **Tradition or translation.** The named art tradition, print process, craft or imaging
   mode that the object is rendered *through*.
   *"…translated into Guna mola reverse appliqué"*, *"rendered as a thermal camera image"*,
   *"a two-drum riso overprint in fluorescent pink and teal"*.
3. **Media and marks.** Name the media and what each one does. These map 1:1 to brushes:
   "soft spray gradients", "wet-in-wet watercolour", "crisp pencil arcs", "flat saturated
   alcohol-marker colour with visible streak overlaps", "soft charcoal-like mass".
4. **Hyperreal hotspots.** The 2–4 places that get obsessive precision: reflection,
   meniscus, label, seam, droplets, frayed strand.
5. **Palette.** Use named pigments and materials, not bare hues: alizarin, burnt sienna,
   cadmium scarlet, cerulean, titanium white, madder, jet black.
6. **Ground and light.** The background, paper or table, plus one light source and its shadow.
7. **Mood line.** A single closing sentence: *"loud, glossy and proud"*, *"hushed, expensive
   and slightly dishevelled"*, *"punchy, cheap-print and intensely appetising"*.

## Scene and landmark requests
When the user names a place or a landmark, keep one hero object in the foreground and
describe the landmark as a loose wash layer behind it ("dissolve into loose wet-in-wet
washes"). Name the paper ground and where it stays bare.

## Subject pool

Pick objects that are ordinary, tactile and specific. Good examples are kitchenware, food,
tools, clothing, signage, vehicles, household appliances and small animals. A worker or a
figure works when it is treated like an object. Avoid epic subjects: the poetry comes from
paying excessive attention to something mundane.

## Tradition pool (from the corpus, extend freely)

Memphis design, Petrykivka, Guna mola, Swedish kurbits, bandhani, sailor's valentine,
riso overprint, screenprint, linocut, woodcut, etching and engraving, cyanotype, blueprint,
botanical plate, field-guide diagram, motion-study plates (Muybridge), thermal camera,
x-ray, photocopier toner, anime background art, lowrider pinstriping, outsider art,
Mondrian or De Stijl grid, Bauhaus, Art Deco, synthwave, isometric diagram, map or chart,
stained glass, mosaic, quilt, embroidery, tapestry, colour-field painting.

## Colour hint

Append exactly one of: `muted earth tones` · `monochrome with one accent` · `cool palette` ·
`warm palette` · `high contrast values` · `soft pastels` · `vivid and saturated` ·
`dusky twilight tones`. The hint is chosen independently of the brief and may pull against it.
Resolve the conflict in the surroundings, the shadows and the light, and keep the named
colours of the object.

## Real examples (abridged)

- **Bowling ball** (`cool palette`). A single bowling ball resting on a polished lane, deep
  candy-apple burgundy over a metal-flake base. Soft spray gradients build glassy depth,
  fine speckle for flake, a sharp mirrored window reflection across the crown. Lowrider
  pinstripes in lime green and cream swell and flick into symmetrical scrolls, following the
  sphere's curvature. Loud, glossy and proud.
- **Sunglasses** (`soft pastels`). Hyperreal tortoiseshell sunglasses folded on a Memphis
  laminate tabletop with squiggles, confetti and terrazzo flecks running off every edge. Flat
  alcohol-marker colour with streak overlaps. Each lens reflects a warped copy of the pattern
  in fine pen. One crisp offset shadow. Playful at a glance, photographic up close.
- **Pocket square** (`vivid and saturated`). An oxblood silk pocket square crumpled on black
  Marquina marble, cropped off two edges. Transparent glazes of alizarin, burnt sienna and
  ultramarine. Paper left bare where the sheen catches. Charcoal mass in the fold interiors.
  Fine grey pencil veins in the marble. Hushed, expensive, dishevelled.
- **Falling cat** (`high contrast values`). A horizontal sequence of frames in the manner of
  an early motion study. Graphite on warm grey paper. A ruled measurement grid behind each
  frame, ghosting trails between frames. Scientific yet comic.
- **Red onion** (`vivid and saturated`). Rings shown as offset translucent vellum sheets, out
  of register, so ghost outlines peek through. Coloured pencil. Registration crosses and
  construction lines at the margins. Drafting-table off-white ground.
- **Watermelon** (`cool palette`). Three wedges translated into Guna mola reverse appliqué:
  nested contour bands in flat bold marker, uneven stitch-like outlines, with hyperreal juice
  beads and seed gloss. Deep orange ground with echoing slits.
- **Tortoise** (`high contrast values`). Drawn huge on brown kraft card with outsider-art
  compulsion: waxy crayon growth rings, hundreds of felt-tip scales, a wobbly hand-ruled border,
  a dense ground of lettuce leaves and dandelions.
- **Red wine** (`cool palette`). An overhead crop into a full glass, read as a colour-field
  canvas. Garnet and rose-madder watercolour bleeds. A single hairline white meniscus
  highlight in hyperreal focus. Meditative, with realism concentrated in a few reflections.
- **Sleeping dog** (`muted earth tones`). Rendered as a thermal camera image: airbrushed
  indigo to magenta to yellow-white, fur direction as fine pencil strands, a temperature bar
  and crosshair in thin white pen. Quiet, intimate, electric.
- **Ramen** (`vivid and saturated`). A two-drum riso overprint in fluorescent pink and teal,
  with plum where the two overlap. Grainy spray stipple and slight misregistration. Each noodle
  kink, oil bead and fishcake swirl is drawn exactly. Steam reserved as bare paper.
